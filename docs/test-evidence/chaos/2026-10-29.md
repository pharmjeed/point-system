# Chaos and self-healing — 2026-10-29 (PNT-098, AC-06 / TC-013 … TC-017, 17 H-03 and §12)

Automated runs of `pnpm ops:chaos` (`infra/chaos/run.mjs`, README in `infra/chaos/`). Every scenario starts from its
own fresh database (`point_chaos_<scenario>_<run>`, migrations + harness seed), its own WhatsApp simulator, api and
worker — the production build (`node apps/api/dist/main.js`, `node apps/worker/dist/main.js`, `NODE_ENV=production`)
as child processes on ports 4120–4150 — breaks one thing on purpose and checks the database, the simulator's record
of what customers received, the processes and the HTTP answers. Integration tests cover the server-side pieces in
CI: `apps/api/test/db-outage.int.test.ts` (SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE during a real database cut) and
`packages/server/test/kill-points.test.ts` (kill points inert unless armed, never in production).

## Observations

1. **AC-06 holds at every kill point (TC-013 … TC-017).** At each of the five points the process died by SIGKILL
   with nothing answered; after the restart nothing was lost — the press stored before the crash became one request,
   the leased W-02 was sent by the next run, the committed accept was replayed from its key twice — and nothing
   operational was repeated: one inbound row, one cycle, one retrieve task, one assignment, one `task.claimed`, one
   W-02 per press, consistency queries at zero. The only repeat the design allows is the customer message of KP-4
   when no status webhook ever arrives (`kp4_silent`): exactly one duplicate W-02 after `CRASH_WAIT_MS`, recorded as
   attempt 2, never a third (12 §5.13 «قد تتكرر رسالة نادرًا»).
2. **Database lost, then refusing connections for 60 s** (`db_outage`, the stop-and-start reading of 17 §12). The
   13 backends of the run were terminated: the next three reads ran on new connections (200, 200, 200) and the next
   press was processed. During the 60 s refusal a read, `/health/ready` and a command answered
   `503 SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` (never 500, nothing kept under the command's key); api and worker stayed up; readiness
   was back 14 ms after connections were allowed again; the same key then ran the command once (201) and replayed it
   after. The press sent during the outage was stored on the simulator's 7th delivery (Meta keeps retrying for
   days), the offer expiry and the escalation that fell due during the outage ran once, after it.
3. **Database frozen 60 s** (`db_freeze`, opt-in: 17 §12 read as `docker pause`) — **3 of 16 checks fail; the gap
   is DEC-PROPOSED-8.** Through a relay that accepted connections and bytes and answered nothing, a read,
   `/health/ready` and a command got no answer within 15 s (the PWA gives up at 10 s, treats the outcome as unknown
   and retries with the same key, 08 §8.1); the api and the worker opened 6 more connections that hung in their
   startup (1814 bytes held). Everything else held: both processes stayed up, readiness answered 10 ms after the
   thaw, the command retried with the same key ran once (201, one duty session), the press made during the freeze
   was delivered on its 4th attempt and stored once (one inbound event, one message, one cycle), the offer expiry
   and the escalation due during the freeze ran once, consistency at zero. What is missing is a bounded wait in the
   database pool: today it has neither a connect nor a read timeout, so the requests wait for the database instead
   of answering `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`.
4. **WhatsApp outage, 10 minutes** (`wa_outage`: Graph `190`, the channel class of 12 §11.4, TC-WA-22).
   `wa_channel_down` (critical) was open and the channel `down` 1 s after the outage began. During the 10 minutes a
   customer request, the accept, key pickup, start and ready, a supervisor’s request on behalf and an exception
   handover all went on, and nothing reached any customer. Within 8 s of the Graph coming back the probe had restored
   `healthy` and resolved the alert, and each ticket received only its current state: T1 (ready) W-05, T2 (delivered
   by exception) W-06, T3 (still requested) W-02; the five intermediate notifications (2 × W-02, 2 × W-04 and
   the W-05 of the exception handover) were skipped `superseded`. T1 was then delivered with the code that arrived
   after the recovery (W-05, then W-06). The same scenario with a 2-minute outage (run `dd34056a`) gave the same
   result.
5. **WhatsApp temporary errors (`131016`) for 180 s while a request arrives every 20 s** (`wa_outage_temporary`).
   The channel turned `down` 121 s after the first failure (the 2-minute streak of 12 §5.14) and `wa_channel_down`
   was raised; accept, key pickup, start and ready went on; after the recovery T1 (ready) received W-05 and no W-04,
   and the requests made from 41 s on received their W-02. The W-02 of the requests made at 1 s and 21 s ran out of
   the 5 attempts of 12 §5.9 (≈ 85 s) before `down` and ended `failed` with `wa_send_failed`: those two customers
   did not get «request received» after the recovery (the supervisor sees the alert and can resend). This is
   DEC-PROPOSED-6 (needs a decision; proposed: defer instead of counting while the channel's failing streak lasts).
6. **Push outage** (`push_outage`): the offer push reached the test Push service through real VAPID web-push
   (TTL 29, Urgency high, Topic set, 513 encrypted bytes). With the service stopped, `push_failed_repeatedly` was
   raised about 21 s later after 3 consecutive failures, counted on the device; the driver accepted the offer
   meanwhile (200) — dispatch does not depend on Push. After the restart a push succeeded, the counter went back to 0
   and the alert resolved itself.
7. **Worker stopped 2 minutes** (`worker_stop`): webhooks were still acknowledged (the intake lives in the api);
   `outbox_lag` (critical) was raised by the api's monitor 33 s after the stop (oldest due row 32 s, threshold 30 s,
   5 rows due); after the restart the backlog drained in 2 s, the press stored during the stop became a request and
   T2's escalation, due during the stop, ran once.
8. **Three workers on one outbox** (`worker_x3`): 883 rows done, split 239 / 313 / 331 between the workers; no row
   completed by two workers (no «lease lost» at completion); each of the 12 customers received each message once.

## Limits

- One api instance; the local PostgreSQL 16 is not a container, so `docker pause` is reproduced by a TCP relay
  (`db_freeze`) and a stop and start by ending the sessions and refusing connections (`db_outage`).
- The WhatsApp provider is the simulator: Graph errors, status webhooks and Meta's redeliveries are simulated
  (8 redeliveries from 1 s, doubling, instead of Meta's days).
- The host is shared with the other build streams (4 vCPU); timings are indicative. 17 §12 and §11 ask for the same
  runs on a production-sized staging before the pilot (owner action, Q-08).
