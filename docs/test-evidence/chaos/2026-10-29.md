# Chaos and self-healing — 2026-10-29 (PNT-098, AC-06 / TC-013 … TC-017, 17 H-03 and §12)

Automated runs of `pnpm ops:chaos` (`infra/chaos/run.mjs`, README in `infra/chaos/`). Every scenario starts from its
own fresh database (`point_chaos_<scenario>_<run>`, migrations + harness seed), its own WhatsApp simulator, api and
worker — the production build (`node apps/api/dist/main.js`, `node apps/worker/dist/main.js`, `NODE_ENV=production`)
as child processes on ports 4120–4150 — breaks one thing on purpose and checks the database, the simulator's record
of what customers received, the processes, the HTTP answers and a device's socket. The server-side pieces are also
integration tests that run in CI: `apps/api/test/db-outage.int.test.ts` (a real database cut: SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE,
the socket keeps its session), `packages/server/test/db-freeze.int.test.ts` and `apps/api/test/db-freeze.int.test.ts`
(a paused database behind a freezing relay: bounded waits), `apps/api/test/wa-sender.int.test.ts` (messages held
through a provider outage, and the bound of that hold), `packages/server/test/kill-points.test.ts` (kill points inert
unless armed, never in production); the kill-point scenarios themselves run in CI on every push to main (job `chaos`).

## Observations

1. **AC-06 holds at every kill point (TC-013 … TC-017).** At each of the five points the process died by SIGKILL
   with nothing answered; after the restart nothing was lost — the press stored before the crash became one request
   (redelivered 3 times), the leased W-02 was sent by the next run, the committed accept was replayed from its key
   twice — and nothing operational was repeated: one inbound row, one cycle, one retrieve task, one assignment, one
   `task.claimed`, one W-02 per press, consistency queries at zero. The only repeat the design allows is the customer
   message of KP-4 when no status webhook ever arrives (`kp4_silent`): exactly one duplicate W-02 after
   `CRASH_WAIT_MS`, recorded as attempt 2, never a third (12 §5.13 «قد تتكرر رسالة نادرًا»). The same scenarios run
   in CI on every push to main.
2. **Database lost, then refusing connections for 60 s** (`db_outage`, 17 §12 read as a stop and a start). The 13
   backends of the run were terminated: the first read met a dead pooled connection (503), the next two ran on new
   connections (200), and the next press was processed. During the 60 s refusal a read, `/health/ready` and a command
   answered `503 SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` with `Retry-After: 2` (never 500, nothing kept under the command's key); a
   device's socket stayed connected, its heartbeat acked `SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE` and it was never told its session was
   revoked; api and worker stayed up; readiness was back 14 ms after connections were allowed again, the same socket's
   heartbeat succeeded, the same key ran the command once (201) and replayed it after. The press sent during the
   outage was stored on the simulator's 7th delivery (Meta keeps retrying for days); the offer expiry and the
   escalation that fell due during the outage ran once, after it.
3. **Database paused for 60 s** (`db_freeze`, 17 §12 read as `docker pause`: a relay that accepts connections and
   bytes and answers nothing). Every wait is bounded (DEC-PROPOSED-8): a read on a pooled connection answered 503 in
   7.0 s (the 5 s statement timeout + 2 s read margin), a command that needed a new connection in 5.0 s (the connect
   timeout), `/health/ready` in 2.0 s, the socket heartbeat ack in 7.0 s — all within the PWA's 10 s, each with
   `Retry-After: 2`, and the device kept its session. The api and the worker opened 29 connections meanwhile, each
   given up by its pool or LISTEN client at the 5 s connect timeout (2316 bytes held by the relay); a connection that
   timed out is destroyed, never handed out again (`packages/server/test/db-freeze.int.test.ts`).
   After the thaw readiness answered in 52 ms, the command retried with the same key ran once (201, one duty session),
   the press made during the freeze was delivered on its 6th attempt and stored once (one inbound event, one message,
   one cycle), the offer expiry and the escalation due during the freeze ran once, consistency at zero.
4. **WhatsApp outage, 10 minutes** (`wa_outage`: Graph `190`, the channel class of 12 §11.4, TC-WA-22).
   `wa_channel_down` (critical) was open and the channel `down` from its first channel-class failure (12 §11.4).
   During the 10 minutes a customer
   request, the accept, key pickup, start and ready, a supervisor's request on behalf and an exception handover all
   went on, and nothing reached any customer. After the Graph came back the probe restored `healthy` and resolved the
   alert, and each ticket received only its current state: T1 (ready) W-05, T2 (delivered by exception) W-06, T3
   (still requested) W-02; the five intermediate notifications (2 × W-02, 2 × W-04 and the W-05 of the exception
   handover) were skipped `superseded`. T1 was then delivered with the code that arrived after the recovery (W-05,
   then W-06).
5. **WhatsApp temporary errors (`131016`) for 180 s while a request arrives every 20 s** (`wa_outage_temporary`).
   The channel turned `down` 121 s after the first failure (the 2-minute streak of 12 §5.14) and `wa_channel_down`
   was raised; accept, key pickup, start and ready went on. After the recovery **every one of the 7 requests**
   received exactly the message of its current state and nothing else: T1 (ready) W-05 — its W-02, held past its 5
   attempts, was then skipped as superseded — and the six still requested W-02, the one made 21 s into the outage
   included (held past its 5 attempts, sent after the recovery). No status message ended `failed`, no
   `wa_send_failed` was raised (DEC-PROPOSED-6). Before the review fix these two early customers got nothing.
6. **Push outage** (`push_outage`): the offer push reached the test Push service through real VAPID web-push
   (TTL 29, Urgency high, Topic set, 513 encrypted bytes). With the service stopped, `push_failed_repeatedly` was
   raised after 3 consecutive failures, counted on the device; the driver accepted the offer meanwhile (200) —
   dispatch does not depend on Push. After the restart a push succeeded, the counter went back to 0 and the alert
   resolved itself.
7. **Worker stopped 2 minutes** (`worker_stop`): webhooks were still acknowledged (the intake lives in the api);
   `outbox_lag` (critical) was raised by the api's monitor 33 s after the stop (oldest due row 32 s, threshold 30 s,
   5 rows due); after the restart the backlog drained in 1 s, the press stored during the stop became a request and
   T2's escalation, due during the stop, ran once.
8. **Three workers on one outbox** (`worker_x3`): 883 rows done, split 283 / 297 / 303 between the workers; no row
   completed by two workers (no «lease lost» at completion); each of the 12 customers received each message once.

## Before the review fixes

The first runs of the day (`dd34056a` on `d86d3e7`, `0aa36699` on `16c3bd1`) reported the 12 scenarios of the time
as passing, but the review found what their checks did not see: the paused database (`db_freeze`, then opt-in) got no
answer at all within 15 s (13/16 — no bounded wait in the pool); `wa_outage_temporary` passed only because its check
left out the requests whose W-02 had failed (made 1 s and 21 s into the outage: 5 attempts in ≈ 85 s ran out before
`down` at 121 s, `wa_send_failed`, nothing after the recovery); the «Retry-After» check never read the header; and no
scenario held a socket through a database outage — where every connected device would have been told its session
was revoked and disconnected. The commits after `2635730` fix the code (DEC-PROPOSED-6, DEC-PROPOSED-8, the socket

## Limits

- One api instance; the local PostgreSQL 16 is not a container, so `docker pause` is reproduced by a TCP relay
  (`db_freeze`) and a stop and start by ending the sessions and refusing connections (`db_outage`).
- The WhatsApp provider is the simulator: Graph errors, status webhooks and Meta's redeliveries are simulated
  (8 redeliveries from 1 s, doubling, instead of Meta's days).
- The host is shared with the other build streams (4 vCPU); timings are indicative. 17 §12 and §11 ask for the same
  runs on a production-sized staging before the pilot (owner action, Q-08).
